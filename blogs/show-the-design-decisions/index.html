Find the thread

A case study can become a timeline of activities: a workshop happened, wireframes appeared, and an interface followed. That sequence says what happened, but may leave the reason for each step unclear.

Choose one thread a reader can follow. In a design-system study, the thread might be a question about repeatable interface decisions. The type specimen, color roles, and spacing examples each support that question. They do not need to imply a larger project history.

Describe a consequential choice

A useful decision has an alternative. A compact layout may keep more work visible while making individual items harder to scan. A larger card may improve legibility while increasing the distance between related information.

Show enough of the alternative to make the tradeoff understandable. A small annotated comparison can do more than a long list of process artifacts. Keep the explanation close to the image it describes.

Make the contribution legible

NN/g’s portfolio guidance emphasizes showing the problem, a designer’s role, the reasoning behind a solution, and what followed. It also encourages scannable case studies. Read the portfolio guidance.

For real team work, distinguish your contribution from the group’s work. If another person led the research or built the prototype, credit that accurately. For an original concept, state that it is a concept and describe only what was actually explored.

Separate evidence from intention

“Designed to reduce confusion” expresses an intention. “Participants completed the task without assistance” reports an observation, and needs context: who participated, what task they attempted, and under what conditions.

The example projects here have not been validated with users. Their closing sections therefore identify a question for a future test instead of presenting invented performance numbers. A clearly stated unknown is more useful than a confident claim without support.

Leave a useful ending

End with what the work established, what remains uncertain, and what a sensible next step would be. A reader should be able to explain the direction without needing to agree with every decision.

Before sharing, ask someone to summarize the case study after a quick scan. If they remember only the visual style, revisit the opening summary and the captions. The goal is to make the reasoning as findable as the finished screen.
