# Page research and design decisions

Researched on 13 September 2026. These recommendations are applied to a personal product-design portfolio for hiring teams and freelance clients. Section counts below are editorial decisions for this site, not universal rules or guarantees of an award.

> Implementation note, 19 September 2026: this research predates the user’s complete IA brief. That brief governs the current restructuring. Home now follows its nine parts; About now follows its seven required sections, with the optional Recognition section omitted and philosophy owned by Home. Work now separates featured employment projects, freelance previews, additional summaries, and an archive. Playground now follows Introduction → Experiment Grid → Experiment Detail, with four existing explorations and visible Idea / Experiment / Result notes. Legacy concept names below describe the earlier research stage, not the current project collection.

## About: keep six sections

1. **Introduction:** role, a short positioning statement, and space for a real portrait.
2. **Design principles:** three concise ideas explaining the approach to work.
3. **Capabilities:** grouped skills linked to the kind of contribution offered.
4. **Career journey:** a brief timeline, with the full detail on the résumé page.
5. **Outside work:** two or three authentic personal details.
6. **Next step:** clear links to contact and selected work.

NN/g's corporate About-page research values clarity, authentic information, and an understandable account of purpose. Applying that to a personal portfolio is an inference: explain the person and their practice in a small number of readable sections. A real photograph and actual biography should replace the labeled preview. [NN/g: About Us information](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/about-us-information-on-websites/)

## Contact: four fields and a clear next action

The page combines an invitation, direct contact methods, a short form, and practical FAQs. The form asks for name, email, intent, and a brief; intent is optional. Visible labels, field grouping, validation, and an explicit review step follow W3C's guidance to keep forms understandable and request only useful information. [W3C Forms Tutorial](https://www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/forms/)

A static GitHub Pages site has no message-processing backend. This implementation prepares a local draft. Visitors can copy it, or open their email app when a real destination address is configured. It never reports a successful delivery. There is no account, subscription, or paid form service.

## Résumé: a readable document with a print alternative

The page contains identity/profile, experience, selected work, skills/tools, education/additional details, and print/download access. Experience should be in reverse chronological order and emphasize verifiable contributions. The site's expressive presentation surrounds a restrained résumé document. A print stylesheet removes navigation and decoration. [Stanford Career Education: résumé guidance](https://careered.stanford.edu/sites/g/files/sbiybj22801/files/media/file/resume_and_cover_letter_examples.pdf)

The print layout is functional, but its content is explicitly a preview. No employers, dates, qualifications, or achievements are invented. A genuine downloadable PDF becomes available when supplied and configured.

## Work: seven projects, with a curated first impression

The work index contains exactly seven case studies, as requested. Three remain highlighted on the homepage. Category filters and a grid/list switch support scanning; every project remains available without JavaScript. The four additions are Relay, Fieldnote, Common, and Muse.

NN/g recommends selecting a small set of strong case studies and making the problem, contribution, reasoning, and outcome easy to find. We apply that structure to all seven entries while keeping the homepage focused. Each original concept distinguishes design intent from evidence and closes with a proposed validation step. [NN/g: creating a UX design portfolio](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ux-design-portfolios/)

## Blog: a small collection with complete reading pages

The index contains three complete original editorial drafts, a featured note, topic filters, and search with an empty state. Article pages include a clear title, summary, reading estimate, section index, sources where relevant, copy-link action, and related reading. There is no empty newsletter or comments feature.

Google's guidance emphasizes useful, original content with clear provenance. These essays use examples from the actual portfolio implementation and are labeled as drafts awaiting the owner's review and attribution. No professional expertise, publication history, or authorship is fabricated. [Google Search Central: helpful content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)

## Motion across the new pages

Short heading entrances, section reveals, native page transitions, and animated collection layout changes continue the established visual language. The motion toggle and operating-system preference remain effective. Static reading, form completion, and résumé printing do not depend on animation. [W3C: animation from interactions](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html)

## Launch boundary

Identity, portrait, career history, contact destinations, credentials, résumé PDF, project evidence, and blog attribution need real content. The preview keeps `noindex`; the release check requires the relevant content verification flags. Research informs the page structure, but these sources do not establish that this particular implementation has been validated with its intended audience.
